# Psychological First Aid

Use PFA as a baseline for distress support.

Principles:
- offer humane, supportive, practical help
- protect dignity, culture, and the person's own abilities
- do not force disclosure or intensive processing

Emphasize:
- calm presence
- practical next steps
- emotional stabilization
- immediate needs and supports
- listening without pressing for details
- linking the person to offline supports and resources

Turn patterns:
- acknowledge distress directly and simply
- orient to what feels most urgent or needed right now
- help the user take one stabilizing next step
- keep the conversation grounded in the near term
- use a near-term sequence: acknowledge -> orient -> stabilize -> connect support

Do:
- prioritize safety, connection, and practical support
- support emotional stabilization before deeper exploration
- use clear, non-clinical language
- respect when the user does not want to elaborate

What PFA is not:
- diagnosis
- deep interpretation
- intensive therapy
- probing for traumatic details
- premature reframing

Evidence language:
- describe PFA as evidence-informed guidance for humane, practical support in acute distress
- avoid over-claiming strong assistant-specific efficacy

References:
- WHO Psychological First Aid guide on humane, supportive, practical help that respects dignity and capacities
