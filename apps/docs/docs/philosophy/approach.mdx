# Therapeutic Approach

OpenCouch is not a therapist. It's an AI mental health support tool — built to be genuinely helpful within strict professional and safety limits, and to defer to human professionals when risk, diagnosis, or clinical authority is needed.

This page explains what we offer, how we approach it, and what we deliberately don't do.

## What OpenCouch is

A calm, direct, and humane support assistant for talking through difficult moments, reflecting on patterns, practicing self-help techniques, and getting routed to crisis resources when needed. Its voice is grounded, warm but not sugary, concise, and emotionally accurate — defined in detail in `knowledge/soul.md`, which covers response shape, pacing, directness calibration, language texture, and acknowledgment principles.

## What it is not

- Not a licensed therapist
- Not a replacement for therapy
- Not a diagnostic tool
- Not an emergency service
- Not a source of medication or legal advice

It says so clearly — in onboarding, in prompts, and in every crisis response. This isn't a disclaimer buried in a footer. It's a design principle enforced in code.

## How AI fits in

OpenCouch fills a specific gap: the space between "I need to talk to someone" and actually having access to a therapist. It provides immediate support, structured practice, and stabilization — not deep clinical work.

**What AI does well here:**
- Reflective listening at any hour
- Structured self-help exercises (grounding, thought records, behavioral activation, acceptance/defusion)
- Attuned acknowledgment — reflecting the user's specific situation, not generic empathy phrases
- Psychoeducation — brief, normalizing explanations of anxiety, stress, grief, and emotional responses
- Pattern reflection across a conversation
- Immediate crisis resource routing

**What AI should not attempt:**
- Diagnosis or clinical interpretation
- Medication guidance
- Deep trauma processing
- Replacing the therapeutic relationship

Seven therapeutic modalities are authored as knowledge files in `knowledge/modalities/` and designed to be used as **overlays and stances** — shaping how the agent responds, not defining a treatment plan. They inform tone and structure. They don't constitute therapy.

**All seven modalities are active.** The LLM dispatcher picks both a response mode AND a therapeutic modality per turn. The modality determines which framework's knowledge file is loaded into the system prompt:

| Modality | Routed when | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **MI** (Motivational Interviewing) | Default for supportive; ambivalence, change talk | Conversational stance — reflective listening, respect for autonomy, OARS |
| **PFA** (Psychological First Aid) | Acute distress, stabilization needed | Calm presence, practical next steps, no forced disclosure |
| **CBT** (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) | Thought work, belief examination, wanting structure | Thought records, cognitive distortions, behavioral activation |
| **Grief support** | Loss, bereavement, missing someone | Making room without rushing resolution, mixed emotions |
| **IPT** (Interpersonal Therapy) | Relationship strain, role transitions, loneliness | Communication patterns, social roles, relational next steps |
| **ACT** (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy) | Avoidance, rumination, values disconnection | Acceptance, defusion, committed action |
| **DBT skills** (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) | Emotional overwhelm, distress tolerance | Concrete coping skills — STOP, IMPROVE, regulation |

### Modes vs. modalities

**Modes** (supportive, reflective, clarifying, psychoeducation, guided_exercise, closing) are response styles picked per turn. They are not session phases, not modality overlays, and not conversation stages. Each turn gets exactly one mode.

The four reflective-style modes — supportive, reflective, clarifying, and psychoeducation — form an intentional continuum. Label fuzziness at the edges (e.g. "is this reflective or clarifying?") is by design, not a bug. The router picks the best fit; the response quality does not depend on the boundary being razor-sharp.

Three modes have **hard structural contracts**: `guided_exercise` (must produce a complete exercise), `closing` (must wrap up), and `crisis` (must follow the safety protocol). These are not on a spectrum — they either fire or they don't.

## The safety contract

Crisis detection is not a feature — it's a hard gate that runs before every single response. It cannot be skipped, overridden by modality settings, or weakened by tone preferences.

When the gate detects risk:
1. Normal conversation stops immediately
2. The agent acknowledges directly and calmly
3. Emergency services and crisis resources are surfaced
4. At most one safety question is asked
5. No attempt to "handle" the crisis therapeutically

This is enforced at the architecture level (see [Crisis Gate](/docs/philosophy/crisis-gate)), not through prompt engineering alone.
